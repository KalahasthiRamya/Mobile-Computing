⚖️ Channel Sharing: FDMA vs TDMA Under Different Traffic

Spectral Budget: 2 MHz per direction (80 nominal channels).
• Option 1 (FDMA): 25 kHz channels $\implies rac{2,000 ext{ kHz}}{25 ext{ kHz}} = 80$ nominal channels.
• Option 2 (TDMA): 200 kHz carriers $ imes$ 8 time slots $\implies 10 ext{ carriers} imes 8 = 80$ nominal channels.

Usable Channels After Overheads:
• FDMA: 2 guard bands of 50 kHz at band edges $\implies 1,900 / 25 = 76$ channels. 2 control channels $\implies \mathbf{74 ext{ usable channels}}$.
• TDMA: 2 carrier guard bands of 100 kHz $\implies 9$ RF carriers $ imes 8 = 72$ slots. Each carrier dedicates slot 0 to BCCH/SCH/RACH signaling $\implies \mathbf{63 ext{ usable traffic slots}}$ (further reduced by guard times and training sequences).

🎯 Design Decision: Campus Telemetry Site

Scenario: 90% of devices send a single 200-byte report once per minute.
Engineering Verdict: Dedicated FDMA or TDMA allocation is COMPLETELY UNJUSTIFIED.
• At 200 bytes per minute, the transmission takes $< 20 ext{ ms}$. Holding a dedicated 25 kHz FDMA channel or TDMA slot for 60 seconds produces a catastrophic 99.96% idle channel waste.
• Recommended Solution: Packet-switched contention access such as Slotted Aloha with PRMA (Packet Reservation Multiple Access) or dynamic CSMA/CA, freeing 90% of spectrum for high-throughput payload services.
