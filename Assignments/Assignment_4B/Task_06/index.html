Inquiry: Why does batching save energy, but continuous high-frequency tracking remains costly?
• Radio Tail Energy: When an LTE modem transmits even a single packet, it transitions from IDLE to CONNECTED state, consuming $pprox 1.2 ext{ Watts}$. After transmission, the RRC state machine keeps the radio in a high-power tail state for 10 to 15 seconds before dropping back to idle.
• Batching 100 records into 1 transmission incurs the 15-second tail cost once. Continuous 1-second transmission forces the radio to stay in the high-power active state permanently.

🚨 Task 6.6 & 6.7: Business Requirement Change & Reflection (2 Marks)

Emergency Mode ("Reliable delivery matters more than battery"):
• Changed Property: Latency SLA drops from minutes to < 5 seconds.
• Strategy: Elevate app to Foreground Service with ongoing persistent notification, acquire temporary high-priority push channel, and bypass Doze mode.
• Trade-offs Accepted: Battery life drops from 10 hours to 3.5 hours; device temperature rises; fleet cellular data cost increases by $8 imes$.
Task 6.7 Technical Reflection:
No single mobile OS or power management feature is optimal for every application because mobile systems are governed by fundamental Pareto trade-offs between throughput, latency, battery life, and computational overhead. A policy optimized for a low-power fitness tracker would paralyze an emergency response ambulance, while an emergency policy would drain a consumer phone before noon.
